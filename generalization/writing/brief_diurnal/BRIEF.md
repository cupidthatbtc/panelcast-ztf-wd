# Research Brief — the 1, 2, 3 c/d (solar-diurnal) trigger family in the D3 negative class

**Date**: 2026-09-01 · **Mode**: deep-research quick brief · **Scope**: literature basis for one interpretive paragraph (AAS 249 abstract/poster). **AI disclosure**: produced with AI-assisted research tools; every quoted claim was checked against the primary text (arXiv/publisher PDF) unless marked [UNVERIFIED]. Nothing here changes the frozen pipeline, the prespecified estimands, or the admitted descriptive decomposition (`reviews/G5prep/sol_diurnal.md`).

## Question

What produces spurious Lomb–Scargle (L-S) periods at exactly 1, 2, 3 cycles/day in sparse ground-based surveys, how do published ZTF/PTF/ATLAS/ASAS-SN/CRTS period searches handle them, what fraction of false periods do they attribute to the family, and can a solar-day locus be astrophysical for Kepler-field stars — i.e., what is the literature basis for calling the 1 c/d family a systematic?

## 1. Mechanism (what the literature actually says)

**The comb comes from night-only sampling.** A single-site survey observes only at night, so its spectral window has "the strong spike in power at a period of 1 day, and related aliases at 1/n days for integer n"; "the strong spike at a period of 1 day indicates that observations are taken near the same time of day: this is typical of a ground-based survey with observations recorded only during the nighttime hours" (VanderPlas 2018, §4.2.1, Fig. 13). Because the observed spectrum is the true spectrum convolved with the window, "a nightly observation pattern ... leads to a window function with a strong diurnal component that causes each frequency signature f0 to be partially aliased at f0 + nδf, for integers n and δf = 1 cycle/day" (VanderPlas 2018, §7.2, Eq. 45). The power that gets aliased to integer c/d need not itself be at integer c/d: any low-frequency content (trends, seasonal offsets, red noise, genuine slow variability) lands at n(1 c/d) ± f_low. Coughlin et al. (2021, §4) say the same of ZTF: the cadence aliases are "convolved with longer term trends, including seasonal and annual variations".

**Solar versus sidereal is a doublet, not a choice.** Dawson & Fabrycky (2010, §2.1–2.2) is the only source in this set that is numerically explicit. For observations gated by "Sun down" and "star up", the window has "peaks at frequencies of n day⁻¹ + m yr⁻¹. In particular, there is a doublet at ν = 1.0000 day⁻¹ and ν = 1.0027 day⁻¹"; the two arise from different gates (night-time = solar day; target visibility = sidereal day), their relative height is set by which gate restricts phase coverage more, and "no peaks in the window function appear between the solar and sidereal frequencies". The separation is exactly 1/yr = 0.002738 c/d, so the sidereal peak is a yearly sideband of the solar one, and every member carries further ±m/yr sidebands.

**What the frozen pipeline does with this.** The alias flag in `scripts/lomb_scargle_common.py` has three parts: (i) spectral-window power ≥ 0.1 evaluated within ±tol of the candidate (family-agnostic but local), (ii) |f − k·f_sid| ≤ tol with f_sid = 1.00273791 c/d (sidereal-only), (iii) offset from a stronger peak by k·f_sid within tol (sidereal-only), with tol = 1.5/T. For T ≈ 2700 d, tol = 5.6×10⁻⁴ c/d, the solar–sidereal separation is 4.9 tol (7.4 resolution elements), and the ±0.020 c/d descriptive band is 36 tol (≈ ±7 yearly sidebands). So solar-day members are excluded from the analytic backstop by construction, and they evade test (i) whenever they sit on a sideband ≥ 0.0027 c/d from the exact integer or the local window power is below 0.1. The pilot loci fit this picture: 0.994 and 1.006 c/d coincide with 1 ∓ 2/365.25 = 0.9945/1.0055 c/d (m = ±2 sidebands) — an inference to be checked in `fp_frequency_distribution.csv`, not a claim. The low pass also subtracts only a global weighted mean (no per-night detrending; `run_lomb_scargle.py` manifest), so nightly trends are not removed before the search.

## 2. How peer surveys handle the family

| Survey / paper | Treatment | Width of the cut or check | Family named as |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZTF SCoPe II — Coughlin+2021 §4, Fig. 11 | Remove frequency bands at the period-finding stage: 0.95–1.05, 1.95–2.05, 2.95–3.05, 3.95–4.05, 0.48–0.52, 0.03–0.04, 45.99–48.01 c/d ("~1% of the frequency range"); chosen iteratively from period-excess histograms | ±0.05 c/d, centred on integers | "fractions and multiples of a sidereal day" |
| ZTF — Chen+2020 §3, §5 | Model aliases as 1/P_a = 1/P_r + 1/P_c, P_c = 0.5, 1, 2 d; exclude e.g. |P − 1| < 0.03 d with poor fit or discordant g/r phase; after classification "aliased periods around one day are almost excluded" | ±0.03 d in period | "one-day sampling cadence" |
| ATLAS — Heinze+2018 §2, §4, Figs 24/26 | Fourier refit at sidereal-day aliases j ∈ {−3…3, ±0.5} with t_sid = 0.99726957 d; residual "slight concentrations of points at periods of 1/3, 1/2, 1, and 2 days are due to aliased long-period variables" | search windows around each alias | "diurnal aliasing that is unavoidable for ground-based observations from a single longitude" |
| ASAS-SN V — Jayasinghe+2020 §3.5 | Tighten T(φ|P), FAP, power and skew criteria for "periods that are close to aliases of a sidereal day (e.g., P ≈ 1 d, P ≈ 2 d, P ≈ 30 d)"; accepts "slight deficits" of real variables there | period-neighbourhood, unstated | "aliases of a sidereal day" |
| ASAS-SN X — Christy+2023 §3–4 | Prob > 0.95 near 1, 2, 29, 60 d; a "discrete spike near P ~ 1 day" survives; diurnal false positives fed to a "JUNK" class; multi-longitude sites give "reduced diurnal aliasing" | — | "diurnal alias" |
| CRTS — Drake+2014 §4 | "removed candidates lying within the range of each period alias" near fractions/multiples of a sidereal day and lunar months | ranges unstated | "sidereal day" |
| ZTF dC stars — McLennan+2026 §3.2 (preprint) | Unit-flux L-S window periodogram on the actual timestamps; discard any peak coincident with a window peak or obvious window alias "using a tolerance of 0.02 d⁻¹ ... wide enough to absorb spectral leakage ... narrow enough not to over-mask" | ±0.02 c/d | "sidereal day and lunar cycles" |

VanderPlas (2018, §7.2–7.3) gives the generic recipe (compute the window as the L-S of unit data, Eq. 48; check |f_peak ± nδf| for n ∈ {1, 2}, δf "generally (1 day)⁻¹ for ground-based surveys"); Kramer et al. (2023) benchmark mask, Monte-Carlo and window methods on ZTF asteroid data with masks near 24, 12, 8, 6 and 4.8 h; van Roestel et al. (2021) defer to Coughlin et al. (2021).

Two points follow. First, the vocabulary is loose: Coughlin, Drake and Jayasinghe say "sidereal day" while cutting bands centred on 1.00, 2.00, 3.00 c/d; Chen, Christy and Kramer say "one-day", "diurnal", "24 h". No catalogue pipeline in this set discriminates 1.0000 from 1.0027 c/d; their bands (0.02–0.05 c/d) cover both members and several yearly sidebands. Second, our frozen veto is narrower and more specific than any peer practice, and the ±0.020 c/d descriptive band sits at the narrow end of the peer range (equal to McLennan+2026, below Chen's ±0.03 and Coughlin's ±0.05). Coughlin+2021 note that "a small excess of sources at the edge of the removed frequency bands remains ... showing that a broader removal could be useful" — leakage past ±0.020 is expected, not a surprise.

## 3. Quantitative precedents (what fractions exist)

- **VanderPlas 2018, §7.2, Fig. 24**: with a LINEAR-like ground-based window, "close to 36% of the [simulated] objects are mis-characterized along these failure modes" (raw L-S peak landing on f0 ± n·1 c/d); harmonics + their aliases add ~15%.
- **Drake+2014, §4**: alias removal reduced periodic candidates from 144 000 to 128 000 (≈ 11% of candidates removed at sidereal-day fractions/multiples and lunar aliases), with "a small fraction (a few percent) of true variables ... also removed".
- **Coughlin+2021, §4**: cuts cover ~1% of the frequency range; without them "a significant fraction receive periods in narrow frequency bands that are otherwise cut" (no number given).
- **Chen+2020, §3**: 1.4 million → 1 million candidates after alias and poor-fit cuts combined (not separable).
- **Kramer+2023, Table 3**: ZTF asteroid period match 64.8% baseline → 65.8% with static masks, 69.0% window method, 70.1% Monte Carlo; "only about 1%–2%" of curated true asteroid periods fall inside the masked ranges.
- **Heinze+2018; Christy+2023; Jayasinghe+2020**: qualitative ("slight/mild concentrations", "discrete spike", "slight deficits").
- **Gap**: none of these reports the quantity our P3 measures — the fraction of *detections on labelled non-variables* that falls in the family. Published fractions concern period *misassignment among variables* or *candidate attrition*; the pilot's ~90% (30/33 confirmed) is not directly comparable to any published number and must not be presented as agreeing with one.

## 4. Honest limits of the "systematic" interpretation

1. **Population, not individual.** The literature's own evidence for the family being spurious is the *narrow excess* at integer c/d in period histograms (Coughlin Fig. 11; Christy's "discrete spike"; Heinze's "concentrations"). The same papers concede that real variables live inside the bands (Drake: "a few percent"; Coughlin: "may have removed the true variables covering these period ranges"; Kramer: 1–2%). A narrow comb is a population signature; membership says nothing about an individual star.
2. **The negative label is blind below 5 d⁻¹.** Murphy+2019 "did not calculate the Fourier transform below 5 d⁻¹, where γ Dor and other variability is often seen" and built the δ Sct/non-δ Sct classes from 5–43.9 d⁻¹ (known eclipsing binaries and ellipsoidals were removed from the sample). `dsct_flag0` therefore constrains nothing at 1–3 c/d. Balona (2013) finds "about 875 (40 per cent)" of Kepler A stars show a rotational-modulation peak whose period distribution matches spectroscopic v sin i; genuine ~1-day rotators exist in this population, and a smooth rotation distribution will place a few per cent of them inside three 0.04 c/d-wide bands by chance (arithmetic, not a citation).
3. **Blending.** In the D3 freeze "essentially every 10″ cone holds more than one ZTF object" (2,244/2,244 flag-0 stars; `data/d3/crossmatch_freeze/README.md`); ZTF's PSF is ~2.0″ on 1.01″ pixels (Bellm+2019a); Kepler-field contamination is documented at Kepler's 3.98″ pixels (12% of KOIs; Coughlin+2014). A neighbour's genuine signal can enter the target's light curve — astrophysical, but not the target's, and at integer c/d only by coincidence.
4. **"Instrumental vs astrophysical" is not the right dichotomy.** Chen+2020's mechanism (1/P_a = 1/P_r + 1/P_c) makes many band members *real slow variables reported at the alias*. Heinze+2018 attribute the 1/3, 1/2, 1, 2 d concentrations explicitly to "aliased long-period variables". The frequency is a sampling artefact even when the variability is not.
5. **Solar vs sidereal attribution is underdetermined by the literature.** Only Dawson & Fabrycky (2010) and Heinze (2018) are numerically explicit, and the taller member depends on phase coverage. Inference (unverified for D3): ZTF observes fields over a wide hour-angle range (Bellm+2019b, Fig. 7) while night gating always restricts solar phase, so a dominant 1.0000 c/d member is plausible; the pipeline's own `window_strength` on the actual D3 timestamps (VanderPlas Eq. 48) settles it and was not computed for this brief.
6. **No ZTF-specific solar-day instrumental systematic is documented** in the sources checked (Masci+2019: ~10 mmag repeatability, no degradation in dense fields). Attribution to *instrumental* causes beyond the sampling window is not supported here.
7. **The band is pilot-informed and post-launch** (`sol_diurnal.md`); any statement must carry that disclosure and no interval.
